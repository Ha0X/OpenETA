# Cloth contact contract

These are current scoped tool contracts, not additional robot capabilities. Live schemas and receipts take precedence.

Geometric proximity/following fields are collision-checker diagnostics, not a force/tactile sensor or proof of grasp. Do not use simulator mesh-derived proximity as privileged task perception. Judge manipulation from permitted public observations; report uncertainty when they are insufficient.

A slight positive finger/cloth gap below the ordinary margin can use bounded separation recovery. The Host checks the whole commanded robot segment against the current cloth with individual positive gap floors, requires predicted separation, then checks measured gaps do not worsen or create new contacts at every physics step. It rejects existing zero/negative gaps, non-finger contacts and unproved escapes. Read `cloth_positive_gap_recovery` feedback; recovery is limited to 40 physics steps per tool call and 1 cm maximum part movement per checked command. A stopped recovery needs a revised direction, not repeated identical attempts.

When `collision_policy.deformables` is `cloth_short_horizon_v1`, the following replaces the rigid-object holding/placement assumptions below. Use the same public RGB-D marks, `move_to`, and gripper tools. A fresh `contact_mark` on an exposed cloth surface authorizes a connected material patch (radius 5 cm) for that hand's fingers only; overlapping layers may be ambiguous. When you intend to pinch overlapping layers locally, set `cloth_contact="local_layers"` with a fresh `contact_mark` on the move or gripper call. This explicitly selects current material within a 5 cm radius, 12 mm half-depth cylinder about that surface along the requested TCP approach (current approach for gripper calls), capped at 0.025 m² of surface. Identities freeze at authorization; later folds do not add layers. Choose single-surface contact by omitting this option or using `surface`. No global cloth pose or rigid attachment is assumed. Normal bending elsewhere on the garment is not rigid-object slip. `cloth_monitor.local_contacts` reports local following and two-finger proximity, not contact force, tension, or confirmed grasp. Verify a lift visually. On `cloth_local_contact_evidence_lost`, inspect/regrasp instead of blindly continuing.

The Host limits each call to a short first segment: up to 12 cm/0.5 rad without a cloth contact grant and 5 cm/0.25 rad with one. Longer goals and waypoint paths return `continuation_required=true`, the effective endpoint, and `reached_execution_target`; `reached_target=false` means the original request remains unfinished, not necessarily that this short segment failed. Use the returned images before deciding the next segment. Do not assume unexecuted waypoints ran. Two hands authorized on the same cloth keep synchronous timing; the Host reports any change from independent coordination. There is no rigid relative-wrist constraint for cloth.

Robot self/inter-arm/rigid-world checks remain. Every physics step checks the current cloth surface against unapproved robot parts and non-table world geometry, and checks robot motion against that measured surface. Future cloth deformation is unknown; a planned rigid-world path is not a full cloth-motion guarantee. Native cloth-table support and self-contact are normal and remain simulated. The rigid `tool_contact` and `support_contact` modes do not apply to this profile. Use ordinary short moves and fresh visual observations for cloth manipulation; report a blocked release/contact instead of treating authorization as whole-body collision permission.
