# Grasp contact contract

These are current scoped tool contracts, not additional robot capabilities. Live schemas and receipts take precedence.

For an elongated grasp region, use two visible depth marks to estimate its axis. Choose the jaw closing axis across that region, with enough material between the finger pads; aligning the jaw axis along a thin handle can give fragile tip contact. Before grasping, if IK rejects an otherwise suitable parallel-jaw pose, explicitly try the same approach and the negated jaw vector before changing the grasp approach. The Host does not automatically choose this symmetry. On failed IK search, `sampled_residuals` can distinguish position and orientation mismatch in the tested joint-limit-valid seeds. These are paired errors for individual samples, not reachable alternative goals or proof that the requested pose is impossible. Repeated sliding or rotation calls for a materially different contact region or jaw alignment, not just another small descent. For handover, leave a visible, reachable solid segment free for the receiving fingers, with room for both grippers. Plan handover backward from loaded tool use: choose the receiving contact region and working wrist pose first, then choose a delivering grasp that leaves that region free. From visible geometry, favor a solid region with broad finger-pad contact and a short moment arm to the intended working contact, while keeping the fingers clear of the working end and nearby surfaces. Present the tool in a compatible working orientation when feasible. A steady free-space hold tests a different load from pushing or scraping; it does not establish a usable working grasp. If contact load rotates the tool despite continued closure, revise the receiving region or presentation before repeating loaded strokes. Contact inside a hole or against a narrow rim can produce closure without stable support.

When lifting a conditionally held body off the table, preserve orientation and XY and use a vertical lift of at most 5 cm. A held body touching the table or within a 0.2 mm support gap uses the same bounded support separation. All convex parts of that one authorized body are checked together against the table; other objects and robot parts retain their normal checks. Read initial_support_state and the scope in host_adjustments. In this mode: the predicted path must separate, measured penetration cannot exceed 1 mm, and measurable separation must progress within 0.25 seconds of simulation with contact lasting at most 0.5 seconds. The live attachment monitor also runs during recovery. Evidence loss or excessive relative motion stops the move because its conditional rigid-grasp prediction is no longer valid. This is not by itself proof of a drop. Inspect the images. If the object still appears supported, repeat the same closed-gripper command and read holding_motion to measure whether relative motion settles while holding the current wrist joint targets. Persistent motion or missing support calls for safe placement or regrasp; TCP arrival and proximity to both fingers do not establish grasp success. Read `attachment_monitor` and `recovery_diagnostics` for the observed failure, rather than repeatedly splitting a failed transport into smaller calls.

Contact with a grasp target requires explicit authorization. Mark a fresh pixel on the target immediately before the contact approach and pass its `mark_id` as `contact_mark` to `move_to` or `gripper_control`. This permits only the selected fingers to touch the selected body. It does not permit the wrist, arm or camera to collide. The authorization remains until the gripper opens or another target is selected. A fresh contact mark is required after motion if you explicitly request a new authorization.

`gripper_control` uses openness 0 for closed and 1 for open. For an unchanged full-closure command, subsequent native actions preserve the closing target instead of restarting a ramp from the measured opening. Read the `gripper_target_continuity` entries in `host_adjustments`; native per-step limits and physical drives remain in effect. This is a command adjustment, not grasp confirmation. Inspect its measured opening, stop feedback and holding_motion. That field measures the recent continuous two-finger-evidence window, reporting duration and maximum relative translation/rotation. For full closure, the Host may continue the same closed command after the opening stabilizes while requiring a continuous 0.4-second window of small relative motion, within a 50-action cap, returning early when the observation criteria are met. Opening and partial commands retain a 25-action cap. This longer observation can reveal slow drift that looks stable over a brief interval; it still does not prove load-bearing support. Read `gripper_settling` and `host_adjustments` for the measured result and added actions; `budget_exhausted` means settling was not established. A missing or very short window is inconclusive; small motion only describes that measured interval. Closure does not prove grasp. Before free-space transport, make a short lift and inspect the object's motion; if it remains on the table, reopen and replan. Once visibly clear of support, repeat the same closed-gripper command to observe sustained holding before a long transport or handover. Stability measured while the table supports the object does not establish free-space holding. If it rotates with the wrist stationary, changing the travel route alone will not repair the grip; use safe placement and a different contact region or jaw alignment. For a task that only requires table-supported pushing or sliding, a successful lift is not a prerequisite: use an explicitly authorized bounded tool_contact stroke and inspect its relative-motion/contact feedback and the images. That verifies only the supported interaction, not a free-space grasp. Avoid repeatedly squeezing or pushing after a collision stop.
