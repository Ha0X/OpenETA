# Push contact contract

These are current scoped tool contracts, not additional robot capabilities. Live schemas and receipts take precedence.

For nonprehensile contact, supply a separate single-arm `move_to` with `push_contact={"body_mark":"fresh_pushed_body_mark","surface_mark":"fresh_table_mark","target_marks":["fresh_workpiece_mark"],"fingers":["finger_1","finger_2"],"patch_radius_m":0.20}`. Choose one or both finger links (finger_1/link7, finger_2/link8). Only those fingers may contact the selected pushed body; they may not contact the table or workpieces. Explicitly marked bodies may contact one another and the local table. Empty target_marks permits pushing/sliding the selected body alone. All marks must be fresh. The radius may be 0.05–0.35m and must contain the selected bodies throughout the stroke.

Each request is a straight TCP segment of at most 5cm in any direction with unchanged orientation, no waypoints/batch, and no simultaneous contact_mark, support_contact or tool_contact. Use short approach, push and withdrawal segments; choose the direction and height from public RGB-D. Objects remain free under native physics and can slide/rotate relative to the hand. The Host does not require closure, lift or two-finger attachment, and does not change the gripper command. Explicitly switching from a previous grasp authorization to push contact revokes that grasp authorization with feedback; a later grasp requires a fresh contact_mark. Another arm's authorization cannot be overridden. Contact permission lasts for this call only, so renew with fresh marks for a further stroke or contact withdrawal before returning to free-space motion.

The Host checks a 3mm TCP path envelope, 0.01rad orientation envelope and 1mm measured collision penetration bounds at each physics step. Unselected robot/body collisions remain checked. Future pushed-body motion is not predicted; contact bounds are not force measurements or proof of sustained contact. Read push_contact, stop and host_adjustments, then judge displacement/alignment from the returned images. TCP target reached does not prove a successful push. Pulling without a grasp requires an actual hook/frictional contact that physics supports; moving the wrist backward does not attach the object or guarantee pulling. This rigid mode is unavailable in the cloth profile.
